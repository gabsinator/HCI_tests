6.) Human Memory
Match the evaluation method to the primary type of human memory it accesses based on the lecture's memory model:
- Interviews asking for recall of a past situation -> Episodic Memory
- Think Aloud Protocol -> Working Memory
- Observation of practiced task execution -> Procedural Memory
- Questionnaires asking for general facts -> Semantic Memory

7.) Iterative design process
Within an iterative design process, what is the specific relationship between a summative evaluation of version 'n' and a formative evaluation of version 'n+1'?
Answer: A summative evaluation of one design often serves as a formative evaluation for the next development step.

8.) Mental Workload
Match the criterion for creating a measure of mental workload to its corresponding methodological concern:
- Reliability -> Same estimate for same task and operator
- Selectivity -> Avoids influence of unrelated factors
- Sensitivity -> Detects changes in task demand
- Diagnosticity -> Indicates cause of workload variation

9.) NASA-TLX
What is the primary methodological purpose of the second step in the NASA Task Load Index (NASA-TLX), which involves 15 pairwise comparisons?
Answer: To weight the workload dimensions according to which factors the participant considers most important for the specific task.

10.) Sampling Strategy
Match the sampling strategy to its correct definition.
- Random Sampling -> Everyone in a list has an equal probability of being selected.
- Stratified Sampling -> Selection ensures participants reflect the distribution (e.g., gender) of the target population.
- Systematic Sampling -> Participants are chosen based on a predefined criterion, such as every 10th person.
- Sample of Convenience -> Participants are selected because they are easily available.

12.) Ubicomp field study
Match the Ubicomp field study type to its corresponding result or goal:
- Experience studies -> Understanding behavioral change over a long period (weeks/months)
- Studies of current behavior -> Better understanding of current technology use and future implications
- Proof-of-concept studies -> Validation of whether a new technology functions in the real world

13.) Validity
If a researcher is concerned that their laboratory conditions do not sufficiently reflect the "real-world" context of the users, which specific type of validity is being questioned?
Answer: Ecological Validity (Note: External Validity is also highly related, but Ecological specifically targets the setting).

- The participant's prior knowledge about the system being tested. (True)
- Environmental factors like room temperature or noise levels during an interview. (True)
